TASK-Q07Y5 was merged as `0829c34` and **reverted the same day** as `0ac88d7`
after it took the operator's daemon down. This task redoes it correctly.

=== The defect

`crates/orgasmic-daemon/src/lib.rs:666` (in the reverted merge):

```rust
match tokio::time::timeout(budgets.connection_drain, serve).await {
```

`serve` is `axum::serve(listener, app).with_graceful_shutdown(...)`. That future
does not represent the drain — it represents **the entire life of the server**,
and completes only after shutdown has been requested and connections have
drained. Wrapping it in a 10 s timeout therefore kills a healthy, serving daemon
exactly 10 seconds after it starts listening, on every boot.

The intent was right and the acceptance criterion was right: the connection
drain must be bounded, or the derived `ExitTimeOut` has an unbounded term. Only
the placement was wrong. Bound the drain that happens **after** the shutdown
signal, not the server's whole lifetime.

=== How it presented, so the next round can recognise it

- Daemon boots, binds, serves normally.
- Exactly 10.00 s later:
  `connection drain did not finish within its budget; aborting remaining
  connections and continuing shutdown budget_secs=10`.
- `graceful_shutdown` runs, process exits, launchd `KeepAlive` restarts it.
- Each restart cycles the rmux LaunchAgent, destroying every live rmux session,
  so **every dispatched worker died 15–35 s after being created**. Three did.
- Replacement daemons then hit the *other* symptom: the predecessor still held
  the home instance lock while its HTTP listener was already closed, so the
  health probe got a connect error and the replacement refused to start with
  "daemon instance lock is held, but the incumbent is not healthy". That is
  TASK-ATAXN's window, and this bug made it fire continuously.

=== Evidence that it was the binary, not launchd

- Booted the LaunchAgent out entirely and ran `orgasmic serve` in the
  foreground: listening at 13:11:19.125, drain-budget error at 13:11:29.128.
  Same 10 s, no launchd involved.
- `curl http://127.0.0.1:4848/api/daemon/status` returned **401** during that
  window — the server was alive and serving, so shutdown had not been signalled.
  The timeout killed a healthy server.
- After the revert, the daemon held one pid and one boot_id across four samples
  over 60 s.

=== Why every gate was green

**No test anywhere runs a daemon for longer than ten seconds.** Daemon tests
spawn an isolated daemon, exercise it in well under a second, and tear it down.
The manager's own full CLI and daemon suites passed on this binary. A defect
that only manifests at t+10 s of uptime was invisible to the entire suite.

That is the acceptance criterion this round must satisfy first.

=== Ask

1. Bound the connection drain **after** the shutdown signal fires, not the
   server future. Keep the derived-`ExitTimeOut` work from the reverted round —
   it was correct and its reviewer-facing rationale still stands.
2. Add a test that would have caught this: a daemon that stays up materially
   longer than `CONNECTION_DRAIN_TIMEOUT` and is still serving afterwards.
   It does not need to be slow — inject a short budget and assert the daemon
   outlives several multiples of it while answering requests.
3. Re-examine every other budget introduced by the reverted round for the same
   category error: is the thing being timed out the thing that is supposed to
   finish, or a future that only completes at shutdown? Say which you checked.

=== Acceptance

- Reintroducing the `timeout(connection_drain, serve)` shape makes the new
  uptime test fail. Prove it by injection, with output.
- The daemon serves continuously for at least several drain budgets, verified
  against a real listener rather than a unit-level mock.
- `ExitTimeOut` is derived again and the plist test still asserts the derived
  value, never a literal.
- A SIGTERM'd daemon with a stuck long-lived connection (ws/PTY/SSE) still
  completes shutdown within the derived budget — the original acceptance, which
  must not be lost in fixing the placement.

=== Non-goals

- Do not solve TASK-ATAXN here. That the lock is held while the listener is down
  is a real and separate defect; this round must simply stop manufacturing that
  window every ten seconds.
- Do not weaken the durable shutdown-loss record (`state/shutdown-loss/<boot_id>.json`,
  fsynced outside the writer). That part of the reverted round was sound.
