* DONE TASK-ATAXN Daemon lock retry is sized for a transient probe, so a restart fails against a shutting-down predecessor :daemon:lifecycle:hardening:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: TASK-ATAXN
:PRIORITY: P0
:WRITE_SCOPE: crates/orgasmic-daemon/**
:END:

Not a duplicate of TASK-870YX, and not TASK-RRVX0 (cancelled as superseded).
870YX sized `DAEMON_LOCK_RETRY_BUDGET` at 125 ms with 10 ms steps, and its
reviewer confirmed that is correct **for the case it was filed about**: a
transient CLI lock probe that holds the lock for microseconds. This task is about
a different holder with a budget three orders of magnitude larger.

** The mismatch, made concrete by TASK-Q07Y5

`orgasmic-daemon` holds its home instance lock until `graceful_shutdown` returns.
Q07Y5 (merged `0829c34`) established what that can cost: `ShutdownBudgets::default().total()`
is 40 s — connection drain 10 + release finalization drain 20 + writer shutdown 10 —
and a writer blocked in a syscall can push the process to the far end of it.

So the same lock has two classes of holder:

| holder | hold time | retry budget needed |
|--------|-----------|---------------------|
| transient CLI probe | microseconds | 125 ms — correct today |
| daemon in graceful shutdown | up to 40 s | 125 ms — fails immediately |

A `daemon restart` whose predecessor is mid-shutdown therefore fails its
replacement start with "instance lock is held", even though the predecessor is
shutting down exactly as designed and the lock will be free shortly. The restart
is reported as a failure and the machine is left with no daemon.

Recorded 2026-07-28 as tx-20260728-orgasmic-3188 from Q07Y5's residual-risk
analysis. Not yet reproduced end-to-end — reproducing it is the first ask.

** Ask

1. **Reproduce it.** Stall a writer so the predecessor sits in `graceful_shutdown`
   past 125 ms, then start a replacement and capture the failure. If it does not
   reproduce, say so and say why — the analysis may be wrong about where the lock
   is released relative to shutdown, and that is a useful finding on its own.
2. **Distinguish the two holders rather than raising one number.** Simply raising
   the budget to 40 s would make the transient-probe case that 870YX fixed hang
   for 40 s on a genuinely-held lock, which is worse than the bug. The incumbent
   classification path already exists (`DaemonInstanceLockHeld` vs
   `DaemonAlreadyRunning`) — the question is whether a shutting-down predecessor
   can be identified as such, and waited for on a budget derived from
   `ShutdownBudgets::default().total()` the way `ExitTimeOut` now is.
3. Whatever budget the shutdown case gets must be **derived**, not a literal, for
   the same reason Q07Y5's reviewer blocked a literal `60`: an unexplained
   constant cannot be shown to still be correct after the thing it was sized
   against changes.

** Acceptance

- A regression that forces a shutting-down predecessor to overlap a replacement
  start, and that would fail if the wait were removed. Prove it by injection.
- A transient-probe hold still resolves in well under a second — 870YX's two
  regressions must still pass unmodified.
- A genuinely stuck predecessor (one that never finishes shutting down) still
  fails fast enough to be actionable, and says which of the two cases it hit.
  "Instance lock is held" without saying by what, and whether that thing is
  leaving, is the diagnostic gap underneath this whole task.

** Non-goals

- Do not revisit the 125 ms transient budget itself. It is reviewed and correct.
- Do not touch the shutdown budgets from Q07Y5. This task consumes that
  derivation; it does not change it.
