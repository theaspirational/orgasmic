* IN_PROGRESS TASK-YPK0D Operator-initiated recovery reattach does not consult blocks_attach(), unlike boot and inventory :daemon:recovery:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: TASK-YPK0D
:IMPLEMENTS: TASK-FZB6T
:PRIORITY: P2
:WRITE_SCOPE: crates/orgasmic-daemon/src/api.rs
:END:

** Description

After TASK-FZB6T.5, two of the three reattach paths consult
=WorktreeAuthority::blocks_attach()=:

- the polled inventory (=api.rs=, =classify_session_files=);
- the boot path (=boot_reattach_candidate=), fixed in TASK-FZB6T.5.

The two OPERATOR-INITIATED routes do not: =execute_run_recover_action= at
=api.rs= ~9180 and ~9481.

The implementer flagged this rather than widening scope on its own, and its
reasoning is worth preserving: those routes take an explicit worktree from the
request or the pending recovery plan rather than from the catalogs recorded
authority, so refusing there is *a product decision about whether an operator may
force a recovery of a run the daemon considers dead* — not the same
consistency-wiring fix the boot path needed.

** The decision to take

Should an explicit operator recovery be able to override a durable tombstone or
an =Unprovable= ledger verdict?

- *If yes* — say so in the code and in the routes docs, so the asymmetry is
  intentional and legible rather than looking like the same oversight that was
  just fixed twice. Consider surfacing the authority verdict in the response so
  the operator is overriding something they can see.
- *If no* — consult =blocks_attach()= there too and return a named refusal that
  distinguishes "this run is durably dead" from an ordinary failure.

Either way the asymmetry should be deliberate. Right now a reader comparing the
three paths cannot tell whether the difference is designed or missed.

** Not urgent

An operator forcing a recovery is an explicit act with a human behind it, which
is materially different from boot auto-reattach or a polled inventory offering a
candidate. That is why this is P2 and was correctly kept out of the blocking
round.
