**`shipped/prompt-studio/conventions/manager-dispatch.org:340-353`** — the site
that matters most. The six-name list and the staged-`-n` sentence are
byte-unchanged; only the middle clause was replaced. Exact new prose:

> *A close does not persist while a sequencer operation owns HEAD* (rebase, am,
> merge, cherry-pick, revert, bisect, or an interrupted revert/cherry-pick
> sequence) or on a detached HEAD: it promotes the files, skips the commit, and
> says why through `CLEANUP_ERROR`. Only one of those aborts actually destroys a
> record — `rebase --abort` takes the commit and the promoted file with it
> (measured), and a rebase detaches HEAD, so the detached-HEAD refusal catches
> that case first. A record commit written inside a revert or cherry-pick is NOT
> destroyed: git declines to rewind a HEAD that moved, so `git revert --abort`
> and `git cherry-pick --abort` exit 0 and the record survives in `HEAD` and on
> disk (measured). Standing down is still the right move, for a different
> reason: the record would otherwise land in the middle of an operation you have
> not finished, on a branch whose shape you are still deciding — and one written
> on a detached HEAD is on no branch at all. Skipping the commit costs a promote
> plus a re-run, and buys a record that enters history once, cleanly, at a point
> you chose. A staged `git revert -n` counts: it leaves `REVERT_HEAD` just as a
> conflicted one does.
